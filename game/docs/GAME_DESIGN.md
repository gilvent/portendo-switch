# Game design

The game is designed with the MDA framework, from the bottom up: **mechanics** (the rules and verbs) are decided first, the **dynamics** (what happens when a person plays with those rules) are built and tuned next, and the **aesthetics** (what the visitor feels) are what the first two are judged by. The visitor meets it the other way round: he feels something first, and only later, if ever, sees the rule behind it.

Each stop has its own design note built the same way. Blibli: [BLIBLI_DESIGN.md](BLIBLI_DESIGN.md). The look of the paper is a separate matter: [ART_BIBLE.md](ART_BIBLE.md).

## What the visitor should feel

| Aesthetic | Wanted | What it means here |
|---|---|---|
| **Discovery** | most | The game is unmarked ground. He pokes at a thing, it answers in a way he did not expect, and the answer tells him something about the work. |
| **Sensation** | most | It feels good in the hand, the eye and the ear: paper that squashes, flips, thumps and flutters the moment he touches it. |
| **Challenge** | least | Only the first minute's figuring out: which button, which way. After that nothing can be failed, lost or got wrong. |
| Narrative | a carrier, not a goal | He comes away knowing what Alvaro built. It is told by the toys, never by text. |
| Fantasy, fellowship, expression, submission | not aimed at | Nothing is built for these. |

## Mechanics: the rules of the whole game

| | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Walk** | Stick, arrows or a tap. He has a little weight: a dip before he sets off, a squash when he pulls up. |
| **A** | One button. In reach of a stop it uses it. Out of reach it is a jump. |
| **B** | Leaves the space; asks once first. At the train doors it turns the destination sign instead. |
| **X** | The route sheet: every stop as a list, for anyone who would rather not walk. |
| **A toy** | A stop with a small number for a state. A press adds one; after the last it starts again. The number lives in the game, so a link, the route sheet and a mashed button agree. |
| **Presence** | Some paper answers where he stands, with no press: doors, the shelf, birds. Never saved. |
| **A knock** | A jump under something hung swings it. Walking past something loose makes it hop. A tap anywhere makes that sheet answer. |
| **Memory** | A toy's state lasts the visit and is seen from every space. The first use of each toy stamps his pass. |
| **The line** | Spaces are stops on a train line. The train car is the hub: its sign chooses the stop, its doors go there. |
| **No failing** | No score, timer, lock or wrong answer, anywhere. |
| **An answer in the same frame** | Every press shows, sounds and (on a phone) buzzes at once; motion follows and can always be interrupted. |

## Dynamics: the play those rules make

Each row is a behaviour the rules are meant to bring out of a visitor, the rules it comes from, and the feeling it serves.

| What he ends up doing | Comes from | Serves |
|---|---|---|
| **Poke and see.** He presses A at whatever stirs, to find out what it does. | one button; toys stir when he comes into reach; an answer in the same frame | Discovery |
| **Press it again.** He repeats a toy because the motion is a pleasure. | toys loop without end; stepped paper motion, sound and buzz on every press; no failing | Sensation |
| **Wander and notice.** He walks about and things happen that he did not ask for. | presence; knocks; ambient paper | Discovery |
| **Follow the thread.** He sees a choice made in one place show up in another, and goes to look for more. | memory across spaces | Discovery |
| **Ride.** He picks a stop, feels the train run and brake, and steps out somewhere new. | the line; the train's speed driving everything loose in the car | Sensation, Discovery |
| **Fill the pass.** He notices the stamp count and looks for what he has missed. | memory; stamps | Discovery |
| ~~Get stuck~~, ~~read~~, ~~wait~~ | removed wherever found: one button, labels only on what is in reach, no step longer than three seconds without something to do | (Challenge kept low) |

## Aesthetics: how they are checked

A feeling cannot be asserted, so each is tied to things that can be counted in a playtest or a test.

| Aesthetic | Check |
|---|---|
| Discovery | In every space at least half of what answers him is not labelled (found by walking, jumping or tapping). The first unprompted answer comes within five seconds of his having control. Something set in one space is visible in another. |
| Sensation | Every press is answered within two frames on three channels: motion, paper bits or light, and sound (plus a buzz on a phone). Nothing in a space is ever fully still. Every sheet answers a tap. |
| Challenge | A first-time visitor reaches the first toy without reading anything but the prompt in front of him. No state exists in which walking or A does nothing. |

## The opening and the hub

The opening is the station platform on the handheld's screen; the hub is the train car on the big screen. Together they are the first twenty seconds, and the place he comes back to between stops.

### Mechanics

**Platform**

| Thing | Rule | Why it is there |
|---|---|---|
| Walk | Left and right only. | The first thing to find out: the stick moves him. |
| Ticket gate | Stops him until A taps his pass. | The second: A uses what is in front of him. The only lock in the game, and it opens on any press. |
| The train | Called by the gate; arrives by itself; its doors take him only once it stands. | Something big happens because of one press. |
| Jump | A away from the gate and the doors is a hop, as everywhere else. | Before this pass A did nothing here: a dead button on the first screen. |
| Hung boards | The name board and the departure board swing when he hops under them. | The first knock, on the first screen. |
| The sleeper | Answers with a "z" as he passes; starts awake when the train comes in or he hops beside him. | The first person who answers. |
| Boarding | A at the open doors docks the console. | Leaving the small screen for the big one. |

**Train car**

| Thing | Rule | Why it is there |
|---|---|---|
| The doors and their sign | The sign shows the next stop. B (or its arrows) turns it; A gets off there. | The hub's one job: choosing where to go. |
| The train's speed | One number, 0 to 1. It pulls away when he boards, cruises, and brakes when he chooses to get off. | Everything loose in the car follows it, so the ride is felt. |
| Loose things | Straps, passengers, the suitcase and he himself lean back as it pulls away and forward as it brakes. | The ride, felt. |
| Hand straps | A hop under a strap knocks it; it swings wide and settles. | A knock that needs no label. |
| Passengers | Each answers when brushed. They also answer the car: when the sign turns, when the chime sounds, when he knocks a strap over their heads. | People who notice what he does. |
| Poster, help point | His name and trade; how to reach him. | The two things a visitor must be able to read. Kept as paper he picks up. |

### Dynamics: what was built

Nothing in the opening or the hub was re-drawn for this. Every change is in what the existing paper does.

| Rule | What it does when played |
|---|---|
| A is a hop on the platform | The first press a visitor makes is answered whatever he is standing at. Landing beside the sleeper startles him awake with a "!". |
| The gate calls the train | The pass beeps; the train is heard before it is seen (a roar that swells for two seconds, then brakes); its wind swings the two hung boards, blows six scraps of paper down the platform and shakes the screen; the sleeper starts; the doors chime as they open. |
| Boarding | The doors slide, the console lands in the dock with a thump, the big screen wakes with a pop. |
| The train's speed drives the car | A low rumble follows the speed, with a double knock at each rail joint in time with the car's jolt. Pulling away, straps and passengers hang back and he leans back; braking, all ten straps and everyone seated are thrown forward and settle, he is tipped toward the front, the brakes squeal, the doors slide and chime. |
| Straps can be knocked | A hop under a strap throws it wide; whoever sits beneath it looks up with a "!". |
| The sign turns | It clicks over, and every other passenger looks up with a "?". |
| A buzz with every knock | On a phone, thumps, taps, the shutter and the brakes are felt as a short buzz. Reduced motion turns it off. |

### Aesthetics: the check

| | Opening | Hub |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery: what answers without a label | the hop, the sleeper (passing and startled), the wind on the boards | ten straps, eight passengers and bags brushed, the passengers answering the sign, the brakes and a knocked strap |
| Discovery: what is labelled | the gate, the doors | the doors and sign, the poster, the help point |
| Sensation: channels on a press | motion, sound, buzz | motion, sound, paper bits, buzz |
| Sensation: is anything ever still | the sleeper breathes, the map's "you are here" pulses | the tunnel, wheels, straps, sway and jolt run with the train; passengers breathe |
| Challenge | one lock (the gate), opened by the only button, with the next thing to do on the departure board | none: the sign names the stop, the label names both buttons |

Checked in `tests/journey.mjs`: A hops on the platform, the startle, the train's wind; a hop knocks one to three straps; pulling away and braking throw all ten; passengers feel the brakes and notice the sign.
